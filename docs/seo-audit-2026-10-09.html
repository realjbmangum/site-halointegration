The core problem

Everything in the critical list below is worth fixing. None of it matters as much as this.

The site is organised by who the customer is. Search demand is organised by what the job is.

Halo sells seven named services: structured cabling, network and Wi-Fi, video surveillance, access control, prop-tech consulting, audio and video, and VoIP phones. Every one of those is a phrase people type into Google with money in hand. Not one of them has a page.

They are cards on /services/. Meanwhile the five pages that do exist target Multi-Family, Hospitality, Enterprise, Digital Solutions and Infrastructure Consulting, which are segment labels and internal vocabulary, not search terms. Nobody searches "infrastructure technology consulting", which is the phrase in the homepage title.

The images are already sitting in public/services/: access-control.jpg, wifi.jpg, voip.jpg, surveillance.jpg, av.jpg, prop-tech.jpg, digital-infrastructure.jpg. Seven service pages are half-built and have never been assembled.

Second half of the same problem: no geography

Halo is a Katy, Texas contractor serving the Houston metro. Low voltage and cabling work is bought locally, and the competitors who show up for these searches say where they are in the title tag. Halo mentions Katy once, on the contact page, and Houston not at all outside project captions. There is no Google Business Profile signal in the markup, no address in structured data, and no city in any title, description or H1.

The practical effect: for "structured cabling Houston" the site has no page, no geographic signal and no schema. It cannot rank for it. It is not losing that search, it is absent from it.

Critical issues

Each of these is independently enough to suppress ranking. All are confirmed against the live production HTML, not inferred from source.

Critical1. Seven live pages serve the same title and meta description

Home, /about/, /services/, /contact/, /projects/ and /videos/ all return Halo Integration | Infrastructure Technology Consulting and the same description. Google has nothing to tell these pages apart, so it picks one and discards the rest.

Root cause, and it is not laziness: every one of those page files starts with "use client". A client component in the App Router cannot export metadata, so Next silently falls back to the root layout for all of them. Adding a metadata export to those files does nothing. Each page needs splitting into a server page that owns the metadata plus a client child that owns the animation.

Evidence curl of all 6 routes returns byte-identical title and description. "use client" present in app/(main)/{about,services,contact,projects,videos}/page.tsx. Only the 5 service sub-pages have their own metadata, and they are server components.
Critical2. A full duplicate of the site is live and indexable at /redesign/

/redesign/ returns HTTP 200 with the same hero, same copy and same components as the homepage, under the title Halo Integration | Redesign Preview. Five routes exist under it. It is not in the sitemap, but it is crawlable, not noindexed, and not canonicalised.

This is the worst kind of duplicate content: a near-exact copy of the page you want to rank, with a weaker title. It was kept deliberately after the redesign went live (noted in progress.txt, March 2026) and then forgotten.

Evidence curl -I https://halointegration.com/redesign/ returns 200. app/redesign/ holds 5 page files and its own layout and globals.
Critical3. No canonical tags and no metadataBase

Zero <link rel="canonical"> on any page. With trailingSlash: true, a static export and Cloudflare Pages all in play, the same page is reachable at more than one URL, and there is nothing telling Google which one counts. No metadataBase in the root layout either, which is also why Open Graph images would not resolve even once one is added.

Evidence No canonical in any fetched page. app/layout.tsx metadata block has no metadataBase, no alternates.
Critical4. No structured data of any kind

No JSON-LD anywhere. For a local contractor this is the cheapest significant win available and it is simply absent. Missing: LocalBusiness with the Katy address and service area, Organization with the logo and social profiles, Service per service page, BreadcrumbList, and VideoObject on /videos/.

Consequence beyond blue links: AI assistants and Google's own local pack lean heavily on this markup. Halo currently gives them nothing machine readable about what it does or where.

Evidence grep for application/ld+json and schema.org across app/ and components/ returns nothing.
Critical5. The homepage H1 says nothing, three times

The homepage ships three separate <h1> tags reading Illuminating, a gradient middle word, and with Excellence. That is the single strongest on-page signal on the site, and it contains no service, no industry and no place. The animation splits the headline into three elements, and each got an h1.

Pattern repeats elsewhere: components/about/company-story.tsx has two.

Evidence Three <h1> in the live homepage HTML. components/redesign/home/hero-section.tsx.
Critical6. Service hero images are 2 MB to 3 MB each, unoptimised, with no lazy loading

next.config.mjs sets images: { unoptimized: true }, which is required for static export but means every image ships exactly as it sits on disk. The service images are raw camera-weight JPEGs: av.jpg 3.0 MB, access-control.jpg 2.4 MB, voip.jpg 2.3 MB, prop-tech.jpg 2.2 MB, wifi.jpg 2.1 MB, surveillance.jpg 1.8 MB.

Eleven assets are additionally rel="preload"ed in the response headers, so they compete for bandwidth during the critical render path. This will be failing Largest Contentful Paint on mobile, which is a direct ranking factor.

Evidence ls -l public/services/. link: response header on / preloads 11 images. 3 loading="lazy" attributes across the whole codebase.
Critical7. 14 MB of assets are shipped twice, plus two zip archives

public/Services Page/ is a byte-for-byte duplicate of public/services/, 14.4 MB, differing only in filename casing and spaces. public/Partner Logos/ duplicates public/partner-logos/ the same way. public/Partner Logos.zip and public/Services Page.zip are also deployed and publicly downloadable.

This is the leftover of the "rename folders to remove spaces" commit (1d33c0c), where the new copies were added and the old ones never deleted. Total public/ is 55 MB.

Evidence du -sh public is 55M. Identical byte sizes across both directory pairs.

Warnings

Real problems, lower blast radius than the list above.

Warning8. Sitemap is hand written, incomplete and missing lastmod

Nine URLs listed, twelve pages live. /projects/ and /videos/ are absent, and /projects/ holds the 18 real closed projects, which is the most persuasive content on the site. No lastmod on any entry. Being a static file, it will drift again the next time a page is added.

Evidence public/sitemap.xml, 9 <url> entries. Both missing routes return 200 live.
Warning9. No Open Graph image, no Twitter card

openGraph declares title, description and type, but no images and no url. No twitter block at all. Every link shared to LinkedIn, in email or in a text message renders as a bare grey rectangle. For a business where Kyle sends the link directly to prospects, this is a visible credibility cost as much as an SEO one.

Evidence app/layout.tsx lines 12 to 16.
Warning10. Eight images have no alt attribute, and the alt text that exists is thin

Eight <img> tags across /projects/, four service sub-pages and three dead components carry no alt at all. Where alt exists it restates the page title (alt="Hospitality technology", alt="Digital solutions") rather than describing the image. Two are empty strings on decorative hero images, which is correct usage.

The 18 project photos are the clearest case: each is a named property in a named city, and the alt text is the one place that could say so.

Evidence 13 <img> tags outside redesign/, 8 with no alt. app/(main)/projects/page.tsx:302.
Warning11. Dead code carries a second, wrong company address

components/contact/contact-info.tsx holds 801 Travis Street, Suite 2101 #826 - Houston, TX 77002, the pre-move Houston address. The whole pre-redesign component tree is orphaned: components/home/, components/about/, components/contact/, components/navigation.tsx, components/footer.tsx.

Checked, and it does not currently render. Nothing imports it, so there is no live address conflict today. It is a trap rather than an active bug: the moment someone reuses one of those components, the site publishes two addresses and local ranking suffers. Worth deleting while the reason is known.

Evidence grep for imports of contact-info, components/home/, components/about/, components/navigation, components/footer returns zero hits.
Warning12. The 5 service sub-pages are off-brand and thin

These are the only pages with their own metadata, and they are pre-redesign: light theme, old Button and Card components, visibly a different website. Known and deferred in May 2026. Their descriptions are also short enough that Google will rewrite them ("Technology solutions for property management and resident experience." is 63 characters against a 150 to 160 target).

Relevant to the restructure below: if the page set is reorganised around services, three of these five pages change job anyway, so the dark rebuild and the SEO work are the same piece of work.

Evidence progress.txt 2026-05-16 gotchas. Metadata exports in all 5 app/(main)/services/*/page.tsx.

Opportunities

Opportunity13. 18 real closed projects, doing almost no work

Twelve multi-family and six hospitality properties, each with a photo, a real name, a city and a unit or room count. This is the hardest content to fake and the easiest to rank: property name searches have almost no competition, and "cabling contractor who did that building" is exactly how this work gets referred.

They currently live as cards in one array on one page with no individual URLs. Eighteen project pages, each with its city, scope and schema, would roughly triple the indexable surface of the site using content that already exists.

Opportunity14. Named partners are unclaimed authority

Verkada, Brivo, Graybar, Best Buy Business, Eagle Eye Networks, OCC, WorldVue, OFS. "Certified Verkada installer Houston" and the equivalents for each are low-competition, high-intent searches, and the logos currently scroll past unlinked with no page naming the relationship. Most of these vendors also run partner directories, which are the kind of inbound link this site has none of.

Opportunity15. /videos/ has 9 YouTube embeds and no VideoObject schema

Nine curated videos from @halo_integration, embedded as bare iframes. With VideoObject markup these become eligible for video rich results. The embeds are also render blocking and would benefit from the lightweight facade pattern, which helps both the page weight problem and the schema one.

What is already right

PassingFoundations that do not need touching
  • robots.txt is correct and points at the sitemap.
  • HTTPS, HTTP/2, clean 200s on every route tested, no redirect chains.
  • lang="en" and a correct viewport meta are set.
  • Consistent trailing slashes, so no mixed-URL duplication from that direction.
  • One H1 per page on all pages except the homepage and the About story component.
  • Responsive implementation throughout, with real mobile breakpoints rather than a scaled desktop layout.

  • The project images were already optimised down from 11 MB to 13 MB PNGs in May. That instinct just never reached public/services/.

Caught in review, after the work above

A code review and a security review were run against the change set. The security review found nothing critical, high or medium. The code review found four real faults, three of which this work introduced.

IntroducedThe collapsed H1 lost its word breaks

Merging three H1s into one left the three lines as adjacent block spans with no whitespace. Anything reading textContent got IlluminatingInfrastructurewith Excellence. Fixed with explicit spaces, verified in the built HTML.

IntroducedAlt text written without looking at the images

Five of the alt strings described images that do not exist. surveillance.jpg is a video management dashboard on a monitor, not a dome camera on a building. prop-tech.jpg is an infographic, not a wall panel. access-control.jpg is a phone unlocking a keypad lock, not a card reader. Every image has now been viewed and its alt text rewritten from what is actually in the frame.

IntroducedCapability and vendor claims with no source

The service page content asserted Verkada and Eagle Eye partnerships, fusion splicing, OTDR testing, firestopping, and that most of the company's work is new construction. None of that appears anywhere the client has published, and Verkada and Eagle Eye are not in the live partner marquee. These strings also feed FAQPage and Service structured data, which would have made them public machine-readable claims about someone else's business. All removed. The file now carries a written content rule and a note about this specific failure.

Pre-existing, now confirmedThe five legacy service pages are visually broken, not just off-brand

They style with light-theme tokens inside a dark layout. Rendered and screenshotted to confirm: the Multi-Family Solutions H1 is near-black text on a near-black background and is effectively invisible, and the section below it fails AA contrast. The May notes called this "off-brand light design". It is worse than that.

Combined with the false capability claims, these five pages are now noindex, follow and removed from the sitemap, and their Service schema has been dropped so no structured data contradicts the page body. That is a holding state, reversible in one line, not a decision about their future.

Also fixed from the review: og:image dimensions were declared 1200x630 for every image including service JPEGs that are not that size; the Organization schema logo pointed at the white-on-transparent mark, which is illegible on white, and now uses the colour logo; public/icon.svg (the Next template default) and app/icon.svg (the Halo mark) both resolved to /icon.svg, so the template one is deleted; a lazy-loading attribute had been added to above-the-fold heroes, which hurts LCP; the contact page now sources its phone, email and address from the same constant as the schema; and public/_redirects sends any indexed /redesign/* URL to its real equivalent instead of a 404.

Two of the code review's claims were checked and rejected: it reported the service titles at 65 to 74 characters and the sitemap at 17 URLs, both read from a stale build. Current state is 49 to 59 characters and 13 URLs.

Confirmed while checking: no off-site presence at all

Searching the company name together with its own services and city returns one conference exhibitor listing and nothing else. No Google Business Profile surfaced, no directory listings, no vendor partner-directory entries. Competing Katy and Houston contractors appear in several. Nothing in the repo verifies Search Console either, though it may be verified by DNS.

This is worth stating plainly: the on-site work above makes the site rankable. It does not create the citations and links that decide who wins a local contractor search. Items 3 and 4 in the list below are now the binding constraint, not the code.
